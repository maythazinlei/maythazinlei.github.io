SYNOPSIS

"Queen of Trash" offers a visceral, unflinching look at the lives of women living on the fringes of Yangon's sprawling waste sites. In a landscape defined by discarded materials and structural neglect, the documentary captures the resilience and dignity of those who sustain themselves through the city's refuse.

This film serves as a critical exploration of social inequality in contemporary Myanmar, moving beyond the surface of poverty to reveal a complex ecosystem of survival, sisterhood, and the silent cost of urban development.

THE APPROACH

Filmed over two years, the project relied on deep immersion into the community. By stripping away traditional narrative tropes, the film allows the subjects to dictate the pace, resulting in a cinematic meditation on labor, environment, and the human spirit in the face of systemic erasure.

"The waste we produce tells a story of who we are; the people who handle it tell the story of who we've forgotten."
